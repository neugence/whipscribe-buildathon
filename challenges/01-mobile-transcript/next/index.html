Transcript reader on a phone — the next pass

Left: the current design (from ../after/). Right: mine, as live HTML you can open on a phone. Every "next" screen is built at real phone width, not drawn.

The one job: someone opens a transcript on their phone to read it or find a moment. Everything that isn't that moved one level down.

1 · The reader

Text stopped being justified (no rivers, no "seg-ment"). Lines are grouped into whole sentences, so a paragraph never ends at "a hard / stop at the top of the hour". The timestamp moved above the paragraph and is a button that plays from there, which gives the text the full width (most visible at 320 px). One header row instead of two; search stays visible, download moved into ⋯ with the other "do something with this file" actions. The preview bar and the player became one dock instead of two stacked bars, and the player is honest when there is no audio.

2 · A meeting with four speakers, at 320 px

The brief asked for it, and there's no current screen for it. Speakers are a coloured initial plus a name on the paragraph's first line, so the text keeps the full width. A new paragraph starts only when the speaker changes or after a long pause. Tapping a name filters to that speaker, which is the most common question about a meeting: "what did Priya say?"

3 · Search, with the keyboard up

The current search pins the match above the list, so the same sentence shows twice and the list doesn't move. Here the list itself scrolls to the match and highlights it. The search box takes the tab row's place, and the dock hides while the keyboard is up, because a 260 px keyboard plus two fixed bars leaves room for about three lines of text.

5 · One "⋯" sheet instead of a 9-item menu, a download sheet and a settings sheet

Search, download and settings were three different kinds of panel. Search stays in place, since you're still reading while you use it. Everything else goes into one sheet with three short groups. Removed: "Home" (the back button already does this), "Sign in" (an account action, not a file action; the preview dock already offers it), and the separate "Timestamps: shown" row (it's a reading setting). Formats are words people use ("Word document", "Subtitles") rather than file extensions, and the less common ones sit behind "More formats". Delete sits apart and asks to confirm.

8 · When it fails

"HTTP 503" is the system's wording, not the user's. Say what happened, whether it's their fault, and what will happen next. Retry happens on its own, the button is there as backup, and the tabs and download are switched off so they don't lead to a second error.
