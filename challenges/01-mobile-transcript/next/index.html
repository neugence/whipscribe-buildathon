The transcript reader, next pass

A proposal for challenge 01. The panel on the left is the current design, exactly as captured in ../after/. The panel on the right is a working prototype at 320 px, built on WhipScribe's own reader tokens. Tap the tabs to move between states; the right-hand panel is real markup, so it can be opened on a phone.

The short version: at 320×640 the current reader spends about 46% of the screen on things that are not the transcript. This pass gets that to about 22%, mostly by deleting rather than adding. The notes underneath say what changed and what I left alone.

What I changed, and why

I measured the current reader on the live site before drawing anything. At a 320×640 viewport the transcript page gives 292 px of 640 to chrome: a 56 px title row, a 40 px meta row, a 48 px tab row, a 45 px preview bar and a 103 px player. The words get the remaining 330 px, and a 45 px timestamp gutter eats into those. Everything below follows from trying to give that space back.

1. Turns instead of segments, which also solves the speakers

The current reader lays out one row per speech-recognition segment, with the time in a fixed left gutter. Two things go wrong. The gutter costs about 14% of a 320 px screen forever, and the segments are cut by the recogniser, not by meaning, so sentences break across rows: in the reference screenshots "several people have a hard" sits at 0:03 and "stop at the top of the hour." sits at 0:07.

The next pass groups segments into speaker turns. Each turn has one header line carrying the name and the start time, then the words run as prose. That single line replaces the gutter and carries the speaker attribution the current reader does not show at all. Per-sentence times are still in the data and still drive click-to-play, they just are not painted as a permanent column.

This matters more than it looks. On the site's own public demo (/view?id=e0394eda…, a three-host podcast linked from the home page) the transcript renders as one unbroken wall with no speaker labels, while the Summary tab knows about "Speaker 0" and "Speaker 1". A reader that cannot tell you who is talking is not finished.

2. The player earns its place, or it is not there

The player is 103 px, a sixth of the screen, and it is frequently dead. It reads "Audio unavailable" in WhipScribe's own reference screenshots, and on the live demo every control is disabled with the note "File not found".

Here it is one 52 px row: play, scrub, elapsed and total. When there is no audio the player is not rendered at all. A quiet line takes its place, says the recording could not be loaded, and offers the one useful action. A disabled transport that occupies a sixth of a phone screen is the worst of both.

3. The preview bar is already saying it twice

This one is not a relocation, it is a deletion. Look at 05-preview-boundary.png: at the point where the words stop there is already an inline note, "End of free preview, 48% read. Sign up free to read, download and keep the rest." And pinned at the bottom of the same screen is a bar saying "You're reading the free preview (first 48%)" with a Keep reading button. The same message, twice, on a 320 px screen, one of them covering the text permanently and stacking a second bar above the player.

So the pinned bar goes. The inline note stays and grows into a proper card with the action on it, because the boundary is a place on the page and that is where the reader is actually asking the question. The text underneath stays visible but faded, so you can see there is more rather than being told. The bottom of the screen is then free, which is what makes the keyboard work in search.

Download moves into this sheet too, so search, download and reading settings stop being three different panel idioms. There is one sheet for things you do to the recording, and search is its own mode.

5. Search becomes a mode

Today the field opens under the tabs and pushes the transcript down while the keyboard comes up from below, squeezing the results into whatever is left. Search now takes the screen: the field replaces the title bar, results are a list of turns with the match in context, and tapping one returns to the reader at that moment. The reader keeps its place, so leaving search puts you back where you were.

6. Left-aligned, 17 px, no hyphens

The live reader sets text-align: justify with hyphens: auto at 15 px in a 243 px column. Justification needs slack to work, and a 243 px column has none, so you get rivers of white space and words broken mid-syllable: the reference screenshots show "be-cause" and the live demo shows "chal-lenging". Ragged right at 17 px with 1.62 leading reads better and costs nothing.

7. The screen that did not exist: still processing

The challenge notes there is no design for a transcript that is not ready. This one shows the same reader shell, with a progress line, an honest estimate, and the turns filling in from the top as they arrive, so the wait is the reading. Summary and Ask are reachable but say what they are waiting for rather than sitting greyed out with no reason given.

What I deliberately kept

  • The palette, the type and the green. Every colour here is read off whipscribe.com: --wt-green #54801e, --wt-text #202a25, --wt-tint #eef5df, Inter. This is a proposal inside the product's system, not a rebrand.
  • Three tabs. Transcript, Summary and Ask are three genuinely different things to want. I renamed "AI Chat" to "Ask" because it is shorter and it is a verb, and because what the user wants is the answer, not the chat.
  • Click a line to play. The best thing about the current reader. It survives the move to turns: each sentence keeps its own time.
  • Timestamps on by default, and still switchable.
  • The desktop layout. I did not change it, and I do not think it needs the same treatment. See below.

The questions the challenge asked

What did someone on a phone come here to do?

Read what was said, or find one thing that was said. Reading is now zero taps and roughly 50% more words per screen. Finding is one tap into a mode that owns the screen instead of a strip fighting the keyboard.

Is the bottom of the screen the right trade, and what about the keyboard?

Two pinned bars is not the right trade. One is. The preview card goes into the flow and the player collapses to 52 px or disappears, so when the keyboard is up in search there is no stack of bars underneath it. That was the deciding argument.

Should search, download and settings feel like one thing?

Download and settings, yes: one sheet. Search, no. It is a different posture: you are hunting, not managing, and it needs the keyboard and the whole screen. Forcing all three into one pattern would be tidiness at the cost of the one that matters most.

Four speakers at 320 px

See the "Four speakers" state. Name and time on one line, a colour per person carried only by the name, and consecutive turns from the same person drop the repeated header. No avatars, no chips, no chat bubbles: at 320 px they would cost more than they explain.

What happens after you select a line?

I left the current selection bar alone. Copy and Play from here are the two right verbs, and the current implementation already strips timestamps from the copied text, which is the hard part and is correct. It did not need a second pass.

Should the desktop page change?

Mostly not. Desktop has room for the rail and the gutter, and the View / Copy / Download toolbar is clear. Two things should follow the phone: speaker turns, because that is a data problem rather than a width problem, and dropping justification. The rest of the desktop layout is doing its job.

What this prototype is not

  • It is a design prototype with fixed sample content, not a build against the API. The timings, names and words are sample data.
  • The player does not play audio. It shows the shape and the height, which is the argument.
  • Summary and Ask are not designed here. This pass is the transcript reader, which is what the challenge asked for.
  • I could not test the signed-in states (rename, delete, folders) because that needs an account and this is a public demo, so the actions sheet shows them in their owner-visible form without a live flow behind them.
